The Skincare & Cosmetics INCI Database for ML, NLP and compliance teams

The 55,426-row ingredient_name_map records how every raw label token was resolved to a canonical INCI name — the resolution method and a confidence on each row — as one offline, auditable artifact for teams training ingredient-normalisation models and for compliance analysts running bulk EU Annex II–VI checks; no free source or live API ships it. Underneath it, a normalised relational snapshot of what is actually printed on cosmetic labels: 18,583 products, 46,973 distinct canonical INCI names, and 318,758 ordered composition links. Enrichment comes from the EU CosIng inventory; where a source has no value, the column is NULL.

A cosmetic label corpus contains far more distinct strings — botanical variants, multilingual spellings, marketing tokens, one-off blends — than any regulatory inventory lists. That long tail is why the left column is low. The slots on a real label are filled overwhelmingly by the ingredients CosIng does cover, which is why the right column is high. If you are building a scanner or a formulation model, the right column governs what your users see; if you need a chemical reference, the left column is the honest ceiling.

99 allergen-flagged names

From the EU Annex III fragrance-allergen list (Regulation (EC) 1223/2009 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2023/1545), matched on exact canonical name. The US FDA has not yet published its MoCRA fragrance-allergen list, so no US flag exists here.

145 comedogenic ratings

Ingredients rated 0–5, transcribed from Fulton JE Jr., J Soc Cosmet Chem 1989;40:321–333 (Table I). NULL everywhere the paper does not reach — absence means "not covered", never "safe".

270 fungal-acne flags

A rule-derived heuristic, not a measured property: C11–C24 fatty acids and their esters, and polysorbates 20/40/60/80, applied only to CosIng-matched ingredients. Basis: DOI 10.3389/fcimb.2020.00112 and DOI 10.1093/femsyr/foaf043.

Data Architecture

Two sources, one documented join

No third-party mirrors, no scraped enrichment, no hand-typed chemistry. Every source file's URL, download timestamp and SHA-256 is recorded in the build_report.json shipped with the data.

Open Data

Open Beauty Facts

The product corpus: barcodes, brands, the source's own category tags and the raw on-pack ingredient declarations that everything else is derived from. Licensed ODbL; the raw label text ships alongside the parse so you can re-derive it.

EU Regulation

European Commission CosIng

The ingredient inventory: functional categories, CAS and EC numbers, chemical descriptions, restrictions, and Annex II–VI membership. Joined on exact canonical name; unmatched names get NULL, not a guess.

EU Regulation

Annex III fragrance allergens

Regulation (EC) 1223/2009 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2023/1545 — the single list behind is_common_allergen, recorded per row in allergen_source.

Engineering

Canonicalisation & the name map

Raw label tokens resolved by an explicit ordered method — exact, cleaned, typo map, percent-stripped, parenthesis-stripped, synonym map, slash variant, split, or unresolved. The method used is shipped per row.

Literature

Authored ratings

A deliberately small, cited layer: comedogenic ratings transcribed from one published table, and a rule-derived fungal-acne flag with its rule text stored per row in rating_source.

Verification

build_report.json

Per-column fill rates, distinct-value counts, match rates and source hashes for this exact snapshot — shipped in the archive so you can check the claims on this page instead of believing them.

Technical Reference

§ FAQ · The questions an evaluator asks

How much of the ingredient data is actually enriched?

Two numbers, both published above. CosIng matches 11.0% of the 46,973 distinct canonical names but 82.5% of the 318,758 ingredient occurrences on labels; functions 10.9% / 81.5%; CAS 8.3% / 76.4%. The long tail of distinct names is botanical variants, multilingual spellings and marketing tokens no inventory lists.

How are raw label tokens turned into canonical names?

By an explicit ordered procedure — entity decode, character strip, whitespace collapse, upper-case, then exact match, cleaned match, typo map, percent stripping, parenthesis stripping, synonym map, slash variant, and concatenation splitting only when both halves match. Anything that fails all of them is recorded as unresolved, never guessed. The method per token ships as the ingredient_name_map table.

Which allergen list are the flags taken from?

One list only: EU Annex III fragrance allergens (Regulation (EC) 1223/2009 as amended by Regulation (EU) 2023/1545), matched on exact canonical name — 99 names flagged, with allergen_source recording the list. The US FDA has not yet published its MoCRA fragrance-allergen list, so this dataset carries no US flag.

Where do the comedogenic ratings and fungal-acne flags come from?

145 ingredients rated 0–5, transcribed from Table I of Fulton JE Jr., J Soc Cosmet Chem 1989;40:321–333; NULL elsewhere. The fungal-acne flag is a rule-derived heuristic, not a measured property: C11–C24 fatty acids and their esters plus polysorbates 20/40/60/80, applied only to CosIng-matched ingredients, giving 270 flags. Use it as a filter, not as a finding.
